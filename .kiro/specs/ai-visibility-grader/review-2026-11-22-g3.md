## V63. Every count queues its own full file write (fix before G4)
`persist` in ai-budget.js is called from every `note` and `once`, and each call queues a full write of the file. The reviewer ran 20,000 refused requests: they took 15.1 seconds to reach the disk, and a `consume` made after them waited behind all of it. A bot that keeps getting refused grows that queue. A restart or deploy during it loses the AI spending counts still waiting, so the day's budget restarts too low and allows extra paid calls. Before G3, a refused request wrote nothing.
- Coalesce the writes: at most one write running and one waiting. The waiting one writes the state as it is when it starts.
- Test: 1,000 `note()` calls make only a few writes, the file ends with the right numbers, and a `consume` after them is on disk too.

## V64. `watch()` must never throw into a request (fix before G4)
`started()` calls `guard.watch()` with no try/catch (grade-guard.js 47), and so does the live-answer route (grade.js 319). If G4's `watch` throws, the visitor gets a 500 after the Grade has already started. The grader's wrapper (grader.js 595-601) catches a throw but not a rejected promise.
- In grade-guard.js, `watch()` itself catches everything, starts any email without waiting for it, attaches a `.catch`, and always returns undefined. `started()` and the route rely on that.
- Tests: with a notifier that throws and with one that rejects, the check still starts (303), the live answer still shows, and nothing is logged beyond one plain line.

## V65. `once()` and the emails (for G4)
`once()` returns true before the flag is on disk and before the email is sent. A crash in between can send a second email after the restart, and a failed email isn't retried that day. Both are acceptable. Say so in the design, and call `once` only at the moment of sending.
