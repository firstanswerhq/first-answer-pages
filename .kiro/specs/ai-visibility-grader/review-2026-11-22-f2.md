## V74. A page with very many links takes too long to read (must fix)
| 1 MB page of | Before F2 | Now |
|---|---|---|
| `<a href=x>` (104,857 links) | 47 ms | 428 ms |
| `<a href="/x">y</a>` (58,254 links) | 57 ms | 264 ms |

The bound is 200 ms. In the `<a>` scan, `reviewsLinkOnPage(href)` is called on every link until 5 are found. For an href that isn't a full address, `new URL` throws inside it, and that is slow.
- Test `/^https:\/\//i` on the href before calling the validator (the same rule as V72).
- Stop looking for review links after the first 2,000 links.
- Add a 1 MB `<a href=x>` case and a 1 MB `<a href="/x">y</a>` case to the timing test.

## V75. Cut the text before normalizing it (needed by F3)
The service-area test needs `normalizeForMatch(facts.text)`, and that alone takes 107 to 226 ms on 1 MB of text. The patterns themselves are fast. Normalize only the first 200,000 characters of the text for the service-area, rating and quoted-comment tests, so a Check stays inside the bound on a big page. Add a timing test with 1 MB of text.

## V76. The "serving" window is too wide
The normalized text has no punctuation, so the 80-character window crosses sentences. "We serve breakfast all day. Visit us at 12 Main St, Nashville, TN" passes as a service area for Nashville, and so does "Now serving lunch in East Nashville". A restaurant could pass the no-storefront check this way.
- Cut the window after "serving", "we serve" and "proudly serve" from 80 characters to 40.
- Tests: "We serve breakfast all day. Visit us at 12 Main St, Nashville, TN" fails for Nashville. "We proudly serve the greater Nashville area" and "Serving Nashville and surrounding areas" still pass.
- If a listed design example no longer fits in 40 characters, say which one and stop, rather than widen the window.
